# Polar Express is the faster corridor route because of posted limits, not distance

On the Harbor Prime–Glacier Outpost corridor, Polar Express (about 34.5 km per carriageway) is about 8 km longer than Harbor Run plus Frost Line through Kestrel Station, because Kestrel sits on the straight line between the two stations. We made Polar Express the faster legal route by dropping Harbor Run, Frost Line and Kestrel Link to a posted limit of 4 while Polar keeps 5, and by counting the Kestrel docking pass-through as time, instead of reshaping Polar's geometry. Routing therefore costs routes in travel time at the posted limit, not distance.

## Consequences

- A driver who speeds on the Kestrel route, which has fewer cameras, can beat a legal Polar run. This is the intended risk choice, not a balance bug.
- Corridor deadlines are derived from the legal Polar time, so changing any posted limit on these roads means re-measuring deadlines.
- Job pay stays based on the physical distance of the fastest legal route, so changing route costs to time does not reshape pay.
